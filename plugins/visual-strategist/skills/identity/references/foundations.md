# Visual identity foundations: critique questions, glossary and reading

## Critique questions

Ask these, in this order, of any reference, competitor or draft:

1. **What is shown?** Describe it plainly before interpreting it.
2. **Which conventions does it use?** Name the category codes, symbols and genres it relies on.
3. **What does it make people believe, and how?** Name the appeal (ethos, pathos, logos) and the levers (composition, colour and light, framing, type, association).
4. **Who is it for, and who does it leave out?**
5. **What is ownable?** Name what someone could recognise from a fragment, and what any competitor could have made.
6. **What survives?** Check it at 16 px, in one colour, on light and dark, in motion, and on the hardest touchpoint in the brief.

## Glossary

**Meaning**

- **Allegory:** an image whose figures stand for abstract ideas.
- **Anchorage and relay:** text that fixes which meaning of an image to read, and text that adds what the image can't say.
- **Attribute:** an object that identifies a figure, such as a saint's key.
- **Connotation:** what an image suggests beyond what it literally shows.
- **Denotation:** what an image literally shows.
- **Emblem:** an image paired with a motto or text so the two make one meaning. In logos, a mark enclosed in a badge or crest.
- **Icon, index, symbol:** a sign that resembles its object, one connected to it by cause or contact, and one that means it only by convention.
- **Iconography:** identifying the conventional subjects and symbols in images.
- **Iconology:** interpreting what those choices reveal about a culture, a maker or an audience.
- **Metonymy:** standing for something by association, such as a crown for a monarchy.
- **Motif:** an element that recurs across a body of work.
- **Signifier and signified:** the form of a sign and the meaning it carries.
- **Synecdoche:** a part standing for the whole, such as a single leaf standing for nature.

**Perception and layout**

- **Figure and ground:** what reads as the object and what reads as the space around it. Strong marks use both on purpose.
- **Grid:** the columns, rows and margins that place elements consistently.
- **Modular scale:** sizes that grow by a fixed ratio, for type or spacing.
- **Prägnanz:** the tendency to see the simplest possible shape.
- **Pre-attentive feature:** a property seen before conscious attention, such as hue, size, orientation, motion or contrast.
- **Simultaneous contrast:** a colour shifting in appearance because of the colours around it.
- **Visual weight:** how strongly an element pulls the eye, set by size, value, saturation, density and isolation.

**Type**

- **Aperture and counter:** the opening of a letter like c or e, and the enclosed space inside a letter like o. Open apertures and large counters read better at small sizes.
- **Kerning and tracking:** spacing adjusted between one pair of letters, and across a whole word or line.
- **Optical size:** a design of a typeface drawn for a particular size range.
- **x-height:** the height of lowercase letters. A tall x-height reads larger at the same point size.

**Identity**

- **Brand architecture:** how several products relate under one brand, from a single master brand through endorsed sub-brands to separate brands.
- **Clear space:** the empty margin a logo always keeps around it.
- **Distinctive brand asset:** a sign that people link to one brand and to no other, such as a colour, shape, character, sound or phrase.
- **Dynamic identity:** a mark that changes by rule while keeping constants people still recognise.
- **Keyline shapes:** the base shapes on an icon grid that keep icons of different forms optically the same size.
- **Lock-up:** a fixed arrangement of a symbol and a wordmark.
- **Shape language:** the geometry a system draws with (round, square, angular, organic) and what each suggests.
- **Spot colour:** a premixed print ink, such as a Pantone colour, used where CMYK can't match the brand colour.

**Colour**

- **Colour roles:** jobs assigned to colours (brand, surface, text, accent, feedback) before any hue is chosen.
- **Gamut:** the range of colours a screen, printer or colour space can show.
- **OKLCH:** a roughly perceptual colour space of lightness, chroma and hue, useful for building even scales.
- **Tint, shade and tone:** a colour mixed with white, with black, and with grey.

**Motion**

- **Anticipation and follow-through:** movement before and after the main action, which makes motion read as physical.
- **Easing:** how speed changes over a movement. Ease-out feels responsive, and ease-in-out feels deliberate.

## Reading

- Alina Wheeler, *Designing Brand Identity*: the process and the deliverables of identity work.
- Jenni Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets*: fame, uniqueness, and how assets are earned.
- Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow*: why distinctiveness matters more than differentiation.
- Erwin Panofsky, *Studies in Iconology*: the three levels of reading an image.
- Roland Barthes, "Rhetoric of the Image": denotation, connotation, anchorage and relay.
- Gunther Kress and Theo van Leeuwen, *Reading Images: The Grammar of Visual Design*: how composition, framing and gaze carry meaning.
- Rudolf Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*: balance, shape, space and the psychology of seeing.
- Colin Ware, *Information Visualization: Perception for Design*: pre-attentive features and visual search.
- Josef Albers, *Interaction of Color*: why colour is always relative.
- Robert Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, and Ellen Lupton, *Thinking with Type*: typography.
- Josef Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*: grids and layout.
- Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, *The Illusion of Life*: the principles of animation behind most motion systems.
- David Airey, *Logo Design Love*: logo process and how marks fail.
